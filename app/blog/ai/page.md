# Don't Let AI Think for You: A 4-Step Framework to Become a Better Developer

> **AI is an incredible tool—but if you rely on it for every problem, you may unintentionally stop developing the one skill that matters most: your ability to think.**

![Cover Image](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/format:webp/1*mkobXBnh-olFpFR4VL3Yfg.png)

---

A few months ago, I realized I had fallen into a dangerous habit.

Every time I encountered a bug, a new feature, or an unfamiliar concept, I opened ChatGPT before I opened my editor.

At first, it felt amazing.

I was shipping features faster than ever. Problems that would normally take hours were solved in minutes.

But after a while, something changed.

I noticed I wasn't really solving problems anymore—I was just copying solutions. Whenever AI wasn't available, I struggled with even simple issues that I should have been able to figure out on my own.

It made me wonder:

> **Am I actually becoming a better developer, or am I just becoming better at prompting AI?**

That question bothered me enough that I decided to change how I learn.

I talked to experienced developers, watched how good engineers approach unfamiliar problems, and studied how people solved programming challenges before AI became everyone's first instinct.

Eventually, I developed a simple four-step process that completely changed how I use AI.

Today, every bug, feature, or challenge goes through these four steps.

It has helped me think more clearly, remember concepts better, and become much more confident when solving problems.

---

## 1. Don't Jump to AI First

The biggest mistake most developers make is opening ChatGPT the moment something breaks.

Instead, search for the problem.

Use resources like:

- Google
- Stack Overflow
- Official Documentation
- Blog Posts
- GitHub Discussions

The goal isn't to find the exact solution.

The goal is to understand the landscape of the problem.

Reading different explanations forces your brain to start forming its own mental model before someone hands you the answer.

![Search Before AI](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/format:webp/1*jzCTj6Q704EzyYYUoNCBuA.png)

---

## 2. Sit With the Problem

After reading articles or Stack Overflow answers, resist the urge to search again.

Instead, try implementing the solution yourself.

- Break things.
- Experiment.
- Change code.
- Read error messages.
- Understand **why** something works—or why it doesn't.

In other words:

> **FAFO (Figure Around and Find Out).**

Give yourself at least **one hour** before asking AI for help.

Sometimes you'll solve it.

Sometimes you won't.

Both outcomes are valuable.

If you solve it yourself, you've strengthened your problem-solving skills.

If you don't, you'll understand exactly where you're stuck—which makes the next step much more effective.

## 3. Learn From First Principles

If you're still stuck, **don't ask AI for the solution yet.**

Instead, ask it to teach you.

### Prompt

```text
You are my first-principles mentor.

Don't give me the solution immediately. Help me understand the problem from the ground up.

Problem:
[Describe your problem here.]

Respond in this order:

1. Explain what I'm trying to achieve.
2. Identify the fundamental concepts involved.
3. Explain those concepts from first principles.
4. Analyze my specific problem step by step.
5. Ask guiding questions instead of giving the answer.
6. Give hints if needed.
7. Only provide the full solution if I explicitly ask for it.
8. End with the general lesson so I can solve similar problems in the future.
```

The beauty of this prompt is that AI becomes a **teacher instead of a code generator**.

Take notes.

Read carefully.

Ask follow-up questions.

Understand every concept before writing a single line of code.

Then try solving the problem yourself.

It will feel slower.

It will also make you a much stronger developer.

---

## 4. Use AI—But There's a Catch

If you've genuinely tried everything and still can't solve it...

Now it's okay to ask AI for the solution.

But don't copy and paste.

Instead:

1. Read the code.
2. Close the AI window.
3. Rewrite the solution yourself.

Writing it forces your brain to process what it's seeing instead of blindly copying it.

Then comes the most important part.

Take the code **you wrote** and ask AI to explain **your version**, line by line.

### Prompt

```text
You are my programming mentor.

I'll give you a piece of code.

Your job is to teach me how it works from first principles, not just tell me what it does.

Code:
[Paste your code here]

Explain it in this order:

1. What problem this code is solving.
2. The core concepts required to understand it.
3. Break the code into logical sections.
4. Explain every line:
   - What it does
   - Why it's needed
   - What happens if it's removed or changed.
   - Any JavaScript, TypeScript, or React concepts involved.
5. Trace the execution flow step by step.
6. Explain how variables change over time.
7. Point out common beginner misconceptions.
8. End with the key lessons I should remember.

Assume I know basic programming but want to deeply understand the code. Don't skip details or say "this is obvious."
```

Keep asking questions until every line makes sense.

Once you truly understand it, try implementing the solution again **without looking at the original code.**

That's where real learning happens.

---

## The Goal Isn't to Avoid AI

AI isn't the enemy.

Using AI isn't cheating.

Using AI **without thinking** is the real problem.

The best developers don't replace their thinking with AI.

They use AI to amplify it.

AI should help you understand concepts faster—not eliminate the struggle that builds real skill.

Struggle is uncomfortable.

But struggle is also where learning happens.

---

## Final Thoughts

I know it's tempting to skip straight to **Step 4**.

It's faster.

It's easier.

And sometimes that's exactly what you should do—especially when you're working professionally and need to ship quickly.

But if you're still learning, building projects, or trying to become a better engineer, don't rob yourself of the opportunity to think.

The first three steps are where real growth happens.

The fourth step is simply there to fill the gaps.

Your goal shouldn't be to become someone who knows how to prompt AI.

Your goal should be to become someone who can solve problems—with AI as a tool, not a crutch.
